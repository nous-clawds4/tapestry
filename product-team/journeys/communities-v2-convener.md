# Journey: The Convener (Phase 2) — empty room to living circle

**Slug:** communities-v2-convener
**Persona:** `product-team/personas/communities-v2-convener.md`
**Date:** 2026-06-06
**Builds on:** `journeys/communities-convener.md` (V1). The V1 journey covered found and fork; this journey covers the middle V1 skipped: making the founded room live.

## Steps

### 1. The empty room (just founded)
- **Trigger:** they have declared a circle. The detail page loads and there is nothing in it.
- **Action:** they look at their own circle, alone.
- **Expected experience:** the circle reads as freshly born and ready, not broken or abandoned. The state is honestly "new," with a clear first thing to do, not a silent void that looks like failure.
- **Emotional state:** proud but exposed. "I made this. Now what? Will anyone come?"

### 2. Seed the people they trust
- **Trigger:** a circle with no people is not real yet.
- **Action:** they bring in a few people they already trust by vouching for them.
- **Expected experience:** the circle gains its first members through vouches, with no approval queue. The founder uses their head start here: as the founder they can seed standing that a brand-new member could not yet earn alone.
- **Emotional state:** empowered. The room has its first faces.

### 3. Reach beyond their own trust (the cold-start moment)
- **Trigger:** the people they personally trust are not enough; the circle needs to grow past the founder's own reach.
- **Action:** they bring in someone who trusts no one in the graph yet, through a foothold path that does not require that person to already be trusted (the discovery brief biases toward an invite that carries a vouch).
- **Expected experience:** an outsider can get a legitimate first foothold *because the founder extended it*, not because an admin approved a queue. This is the founder's growth engine and the Newcomer's entry seen from the founder's side.
- **Emotional state:** relieved that growth is possible without becoming a gatekeeper; a little careful, because extending a foothold is a real act of trust.

### 4. Generate the first signs of life
- **Trigger:** members are present but nothing is happening; the room is quiet.
- **Action:** they post, and members reply and react; activity begins to accumulate.
- **Expected experience:** the circle starts to *read as alive* to a visitor: there is conversation, there are faces, there is recent activity. Members can be reached when something involving them happens (such as being vouched for) on terms they control. Every one of these aliveness mechanisms honours member sovereignty: pulled and configurable, never scheduled at them.
- **Emotional state:** encouraged. "This is starting to feel like a place, not a page."

### 5. The head start decays
- **Trigger:** the trust graph inside the circle fills in; members are vouching for each other independent of the founder.
- **Action:** the founder does nothing special; the system quietly reduces their elevated standing toward that of any peer.
- **Expected experience:** the founder can *see* that their special standing is fading on a legible rule, and that the circle increasingly runs itself. The decay is visible and trustworthy, not a silent backend change.
- **Emotional state:** a complicated relief. Letting go is the point, and the product is proving it meant it.

### 6. Tend as a peer, including caretaking
- **Trigger:** norms need light tending; a bad actor appears; an old or dead circle needs retiring.
- **Action:** the founder participates and vouches like any member, and caretaking acts (handling a bad actor, retiring a circle) happen as trust-weighted community acts rather than founder buttons.
- **Expected experience:** the circle stays healthy without an admin. Retiring a circle and dealing with harm are legible community mechanisms, not owner powers. (Includes the concrete near-term need to retire three legacy test circles.)
- **Emotional state:** lighter. The weight of sole responsibility is gone, and the circle did not need them to be a cop.

### 7. Fork or step back
- **Trigger:** the circle outgrows them or heads somewhere they disagree with.
- **Action:** they fork the definition, carrying forward the people who agree, or simply step back.
- **Expected experience:** continuity without ownership; the circle survives a fork or their departure. (Mechanic shipped in V1; included here to close the arc.)
- **Emotional state:** free. Proof the circle was never a hostage to them.

## Friction points
- **Dead-room despair (step 1):** if a freshly founded circle looks identical to an abandoned one, founders conclude they failed and leave before seeding. The "new and ready" state must be unmistakable and must point at a first action.
- **Cold-start stall (step 3):** if the only people a founder can bring in must already be trusted, the circle cannot grow past the founder's own network and dies at a handful of members. The outsider-foothold path is the make-or-break of the whole arc.
- **Owner-creep temptation (steps 3, 6):** inviting people in and retiring circles are exactly the acts that pull toward an admin model. If these are built as founder powers rather than trust-weighted acts, Phase 2 quietly rebuilds the owner the product rejects.
- **Extraction temptation (step 4):** the obvious way to make a room feel alive is the owned-platform notification-and-feed loop. If aliveness fails the sovereignty test, the product betrays its members to look busy.
- **Invisible decay (step 5):** if the head start fades silently, founders cannot tell whether they are still special, and the no-owner promise becomes unfalsifiable and therefore untrusted. Decay must be legible.
