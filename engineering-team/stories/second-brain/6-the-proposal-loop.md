# Story 6: The proposal loop — nominate one viable goal, decide it on the spine

**Status:** Draft
**Created:** 2026-07-24
**Type:** Feature
**Epic:** `second-brain` (#6) · PRD §5.5, §5.8, §7.1–7.2, §7.7

## Background
Deciding what deserves the next unit of attention is the owner's Big Challenge, and today it happens ad hoc, in their head (PRD §1). Stories 1–5 built the substrate that makes a better loop possible: goals live in the brain, decomposed into session-sized **viable** pieces (a leaf with both a deliverable and a boundary — story 3), pointed at the world (story 4), and every session now orients from the brain and leaves an append-only record on the one spine (story 5). Story 5 built the goal-detail **`records[]`** projection as an explicit extension seam and shipped exactly the `worked` / `noted` record types, leaving `proposed` / `approved` / `skipped` for this story.

**Story 6 is the product's heart:** it moves "what next?" out of the owner's head into an **auditable, teachable loop** (PRD §5.5, owner journey 3). The system nominates exactly one viable goal with a why-now the owner can read in seconds and the named runners-up it passed over, each with a one-line why-not. The owner **approves** (and, in v1, launches the session themselves) or **skips with a one-line reason**. Every decision is a dated, append-only fact on the nominated goal's spine; nothing is ever decided silently. This is the **first brain view with owner-facing action controls that write** — the third v1 view (Proposal queue), the design guide's emphasis card with equal-weight Approve / Skip-with-reason.

Why the record's completeness is load-bearing: the accumulated decisions are the **calibration corpus** the Phase-2→3 entry metric counts (proposal→launch agreement ≥50% over ≥15 decisions — PRD §10). If the record is incomplete or a decision is silent, the metric cannot be computed honestly. The decision record's completeness is therefore the point of the story, not a side effect.

Affected: **The Delegating Owner** (feels *in command* — journey 3; the loop that makes delegation trustworthy). The machine persona is not the justification here (traceability guard satisfied by owner journey 3).

## User-facing description
As the owner, I want the system to periodically put exactly one viable goal in front of me — with why it's the next thing and the named alternatives it set aside, each with a one-line reason — so that I can approve it (and launch it myself) or skip it with a one-line reason, with every choice recorded on the goal it was about, so that choosing what to do next stops living only in my head and becomes an auditable, teachable ledger I can trust — and nothing is ever decided for me without my seeing it.

## Acceptance criteria
Testable from the outside (input → observable behavior). Owner-facing strings are **verbatim** from `product-team/guides/second-brain-style-guide.md`; the Proposal card / queue / Skip-with-reason control follow `product-team/guides/second-brain-design-guide.md` (+ wireframes §3), binding at review. Consistent with the epic's settled write pattern (stories 3/4/5): brain **writes** are gated, validated before any write, serialized, and local-only. Story 6's departure from that pattern is deliberate and scoped in AC2/Open questions: the **decide** actions are triggered from an owner-facing control (the Approve / Skip buttons), not conversation.

- [ ] **AC1 — A proposal nominates exactly one viable goal, with comparative rationale in the canonical register.** Given a brain that holds viable leaf goals, when a proposal is produced, then it nominates **exactly one** goal, that goal is **viable** (a leaf carrying both a deliverable and a boundary — story-3 "viable"), and it is **never** a goal with children and **never** a non-viable / `captured` / `achieved` / `abandoned` goal. The proposal carries a **why-now** in one or two plain-language sentences, and **named runners-up** — the goals it passed over — **each with a one-line why-not**, following the style guide's canonical skeleton verbatim: *"Next: {goal}."* + why-now + *"considered instead"* + one line per runner-up. *(Testable: a produced proposal references exactly one goal id and that goal is viable-leaf; a proposal that would nominate a parent or non-viable goal is not produced / is refused; each named runner-up is a distinct real goal in the brain, each with a non-empty why-not.)*

- [ ] **AC2 — The Proposal queue view renders open proposals as emphasis cards with equal-weight Approve and Skip.** Given one or more **open** proposals, when the owner opens the **Proposal queue** (the third v1 view — a route in the owner-gated control panel, reachable from the brain navigation exactly as the Goals view is, and never visitor-facing), then each open proposal renders as the design guide's **emphasis card** — the one permitted derived accent tint, *"Next: {goal}"*, the why-now as body text, and a *"considered instead"* block listing exactly the passed-over goals (name + muted why-not, one line each) — with **two equal-weight buttons: Approve** (solid) and **Skip…** (outline, the ellipsis signalling a reason is coming). Open proposals sort newest-first; a decided proposal never renders in the queue again. With **no open proposal**, the view shows the canonical empty state verbatim: *"No proposal right now — the next one appears when there are viable goals to choose between."* When the proposer could not run, the view shows the canonical error verbatim: *"The proposer couldn't run — its last message: {plain-English reason}. Nothing was decided for you."* *(Testable: the route exists and is owner-gated (a non-owner remote caller is refused, mirroring the Goals view); an open proposal renders the named parts and both actions; neither action is visually subordinate to the other; empty and error states render the verbatim strings.)*

- [ ] **AC3 — Approve records a dated `approved` decision, retires the proposal, and appears on the goal's spine; the owner launches.** Given an open proposal, when the owner approves it, then an **`approved`** decision is recorded **with its date**, the proposal is **no longer open** (it leaves the queue and never renders there again), and an **`approved`** entry appears on the **nominated goal's record spine** (the goal detail's append-only record section). In v1, **approve launches nothing** — the owner launches the session themselves; the approve action has no code path that starts work or spawns a session (PRD §5.5, §7.3). The in-view confirmation is one quiet plain sentence (see Open questions #4 — the verbatim approve string is not yet pinned in the style guide). *(Testable: after approve, the proposal is absent from the queue; the nominated goal's detail shows a dated `approved` record; no session/launch side effect is triggered by approve.)*

- [ ] **AC4 — Skip requires a one-line reason, records the skip and reason, and confirms with the canonical sentence.** Given an open proposal, when the owner chooses **Skip…**, then a **one-line reason is required before the skip can complete** — the Skip control stays **disabled until the reason is non-empty** (placeholder verbatim: *"why not this one, in a few words"*) — and on submit the **`skipped`** decision **and its reason** are recorded with their date, the proposal leaves the queue, a **`skipped`** entry (carrying the reason) appears on the **nominated goal's spine**, and the confirmation is the canonical sentence verbatim: *"Skipped — noted."* *(Testable: a skip with an empty reason cannot complete / is refused; a skip with a reason records the reason on the goal's spine as a dated `skipped` entry and confirms with the verbatim string.)*

- [ ] **AC5 — Nothing is decided silently; every proposal is open / approved / skipped, legible on the one spine; the record is append-only.** Given any proposal, its standing is **exactly one** of the canonical words **`open`**, **`approved`**, or **`skipped`** (no synonyms, no invented states, no fourth state) and it transitions **only** by an owner act — there is **no** path that decides a proposal automatically, and **no auto-expiry** in v1 (an open proposal stays open until the owner decides it). The **making** of a proposal is itself a recorded fact visible on the nominated goal's spine as a **`proposed`** entry (design guide record type), and the decision adds the `approved` / `skipped` entry — so a proposal's full lifecycle is legible on the goal's **one spine** (design principle 3), never assembled across surfaces. Record entries are **append-only**: no edit or delete affordance exists on any record entry, and deciding a proposal never rewrites a record entry the owner has already seen (PRD §7.2 — corrections are new facts). *(Testable: the standing word set is exactly the canonical three; no code path transitions a proposal to decided without an owner action; a decided proposal's outcome is on the goal detail; the record section exposes no edit/delete affordance.)*

- [ ] **AC6 — No numeric score in any owner-facing proposal copy.** Given every owner-facing proposal string — the card, the why-now, the runner-up why-nots, the *"considered instead"* block, the confirmations, and the empty/error states — **no numeric score, percentage, gauge, star rating, or ranking number appears anywhere**. Relative value is carried by **comparisons and words only** (design principle 2; style guide "comparisons, not decimals"). *(Testable: a scan of the Proposal view's owner-facing strings and rendered proposal contents finds no numeric-score token; runner-ups are ordered/justified in words, not by a shown number.)*

- [ ] **AC7 — Copy discipline, the append-only spine, second-operator guard, and no regression.** Every owner-facing string this story adds — the `proposed` / `approved` / `skipped` type words, the proposal register chrome, the skip prompt and confirmation, the empty and error states, and any decision-attribution wording — passes the **banned-jargon scan** (*element, kind, schema, event, pubkey, superset, concept header, persona, acceptance criteria, lease, payload, endpoint*), carries **no exclamation marks**, and comes **verbatim** from the guides where a canonical string exists (the story-3/4/5 jargon scan extends over the new Proposal view and the extended record types). The Proposal concept is **new, append-only, runtime-created, never firmware-seeded**, with the assistant identity **resolved at runtime, never hardcoded** (PRD §7.8; the empty brain still offers its one obvious action — second-operator guard §5.9). The brain surfaces stories 1–5 shipped continue to pass their suites, amended only where this story legitimately extends them (e.g. the goal-detail `records[]` projection now also carries `proposed` / `approved` / `skipped`).

- **Proposal** — **new concept**, **append-only**, runtime-created on the graph's established pointer-element pattern (PRD §6), **never firmware-seeded** (PRD §7.8). Plain-language fields (PRD §6): the **nominated goal**, the **why-now**, the **passed-over goals each with a why-not**, a **made-on** date; a **decision** (`open` → `approved` | `skipped`), a **decision-reason** (**required on skip**), and a **decided-on** date. *(Confirmed absent from the live graph 2026-07-24 — 52 concepts present, 0 proposal concepts. The Architect resolves the handle/slug and the self-bootstrap sequence, mirroring story 5's Work Record and story 4's External Resource provisioning precedents.)*
- Relationships **Proposal → nominates → Goal** and **Proposal → passed over → Goal** — **record-based** (durable facts held in the record and rendered from records; **no** relationship-whitelist edge), following the story-3/4/5 precedent. This story does **not** extend the relationship-primitive whitelist.

## Out of scope
- **The proposer's cadence, schedule, or automatic triggering.** PRD §5.5 says "periodically"; the queue note makes cadence and the selection heuristic **deliberately unspecified** — the Architect picks the *simplest honest mechanism* (Open questions #2). No autonomous scheduler/metabolism is built here (that is Phase 2 — PRD §8.3).
- **Proposal auto-expiry / queue-age.** An open proposal stays open until the owner decides it. Expiry and the median-open-proposal-age metric are **Phase 2** (PRD §8.3; queue note explicit).
- **The selection algorithm's sophistication.** Beyond "nominates one viable goal with comparative rationale, with real passed-over runners-up," no ranking model, scoring, or priority-signal consumption is required — **priority signals are story 7** (a proposal's why-now *may* cite them later, but story 7 builds the signals; story 6 does not depend on them).
- **Autonomous launch.** Approve **records** an approved decision; the **owner** launches the session themselves in v1 (PRD §5.5). No brain-side "launch answer," claim/lease, or scheduler write-back (all **Phase 2/3** — PRD §8.3).
- **Editing / deleting / retracting a proposal or a decision.** Append-only; a correction is a new recorded fact (PRD §7.2).
- **Re-opening a decided proposal, or proposing a goal with children / a non-viable goal.** A parent goal is never proposed (PRD §5.2); a decided proposal never returns to the queue.
- **The morning-review digest / any cross-goal roll-up** — Phase 3. Proposals feed it later; no digest now.
- **A mechanism-enforced private mode.** Privacy stays the v1 convention (local-only, no outbound sync; the indicator line, never a toggle — PRD §7.4).

## Open questions
Resolved from the queue + PRD + guides + the story-6 handoff:
- Record type words this story adds live: **`proposed`** (a proposal was made), **`approved`**, **`skipped`** (its decision). These merge into story 5's existing `records[]` projection; the RecordEntry UI already renders any type word.
- Dependencies **stories 3 and 5** are **Done and in production** — satisfied (viable goals exist; decisions and records share the spine).
- Owner-facing copy is verbatim from the style guide (register, skip prompt, "Skipped — noted.", empty state, error state); **no numeric score** anywhere owner-facing.

Deferred to the Architect (mechanism / value, not intent):
1. **THE load-bearing design question — the `open → approved | skipped` transition vs. strict append-only (PRD §7.2).** Work records are *strictly* append-only (story 5). A **Proposal changes state** (open → decided). Two shapes to weigh (handoff): **(a)** mutate the proposal element's `decision` field in place — a *durable-intent update*, the way story 4's `verify-resource` overwrites `lastVerified` (permissible because §7.2 governs the **record entities**, not all metadata); or **(b)** keep the proposal element immutable and append a **separate decision fact** (an `approved`/`skipped` record referencing the proposal), deriving "is this proposal still open?" at read from whether a decision fact exists. **(b)** is more consistent with the append-only spine and gives the goal's `approved`/`skipped` spine entries for free, at the cost of a read-time join; **(a)** is simpler but reintroduces a mutable element. **The intent is fixed** (a decision is a dated, owner-made, append-only fact; the spine is never rewritten under the owner; nothing is decided silently); the shape is the Architect's — this is the story's spine, as the (goal, locator) identity was story 4's and bounded orientation was story 5's.
2. **Cadence & selection heuristic** — how and when a proposal is produced, and how the one viable goal + its runners-up are chosen, are **deliberately unspecified product-side** (queue note). The Architect chooses the *simplest honest mechanism* (e.g. an owner/loopback-triggered "make a proposal" primitive the conversational agent invokes, choosing among viable leaves). The **intent** — exactly one viable goal, a legible why-now, real named passed-over runners-up, no numeric score — is fixed; the mechanism is the Architect's, and the Tester must be able to demonstrate the contract from outside.
3. **Is a `proposed` entry emitted on the goal's spine at make-time (confirm at gate)?** The design guide lists `proposed` as a record type and design principle 3 ("one spine") implies the making of a proposal renders on the nominated goal's spine. Default carried in AC5: **yes** — making a proposal emits a `proposed` spine entry, and the decision adds `approved`/`skipped`, so the full lifecycle is legible on the one spine. (Under transition-shape (b) this is nearly free; under (a) it is derived from the element's state.)
4. **The approve confirmation sentence is not pinned verbatim in the style guide.** The guide gives canonical confirmations for capture, skip, and restore, but **not** approve. The design guide asks only for "one quiet sentence" that signals the owner launches it themselves (PRD §5.5). A canonical approve string must be **authored to register** (calm, brief, second person, jargon-clean, no exclamation, ≤ one sentence) and ratified at a gate — it should not be invented ad hoc at implementation. *(This is a style-guide gap surfaced by story 6.)* **Operator ruling at the planning gate (2026-07-24): deferred to a gate** — the Architect/Tester brings a proposed approve string for operator ratification (Architecture or Test-Design gate), rather than settling it now.
5. **The interactive-view write is a deliberate departure from the epic's "no new UI form" pattern.** Stories 1/3/4/5 kept writes in conversation with the Goal detail as display-only. Story 6's Approve / Skip are **owner-facing controls that write** (the design guide mandates the interactive card). The write path still rides the settled **gated + validated + serialized + local-only** discipline; only the **trigger** is new. Confirm the owner-gating on these action controls matches the Goals-view gating (owner / loopback; a non-owner cannot decide a proposal).
